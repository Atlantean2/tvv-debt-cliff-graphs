These are hand-offs for Risk Watcher. The PNGs are in `debt-site/assets/`, each 4800×2700 at 300 dpi; the smallest text is 16 pt. They were made by `graphs/src/tvv site adds 2026-10-30 dc debt cyber.py`, and the raw data is saved in `graphs/src/tvv site adds 2026-10-30 data/`. index.html and build.py were not touched.

## 1. `Marketable Treasury debt by type 2001-2026.png` → **Q1 US Maturity Wall**
- **Title:** Marketable Treasury debt hits $31.8T; bills alone are $7.1T
- **Caption:** US marketable Treasury debt by security type, monthly to Sep 2026; bills are 22%. Source: Treasury MSPD.
- **Label:** DATA
- **Bullets**
  - Sep 30 2026: $31.83T in marketable debt, made up of bills $7.12T, notes $16.28T, bonds $5.55T, TIPS $2.17T and FRNs $0.71T.
  - Jan 2001: $2.8T. That is about 11 times as much in 25 years.
  - The bills ($7.1T, 22%) roll over within 12 months and reprice at current short rates. This chart complements the existing DC1 wall, which shows $10.6T due within 12 months.
- **Sources**
  - https://fiscaldata.treasury.gov/datasets/monthly-statement-public-debt/summary-of-treasury-securities-outstanding
  - https://api.fiscaldata.treasury.gov/services/api/fiscal_service/v1/debt/mspd/mspd_table_1
- **Doubt notes**
  - "The bill share is normal; Treasury's guidance is 15–20%, and it was higher in 2008–09." That's true. The point is the dollar size of the stock that reprices. TBAC: https://home.treasury.gov/policy-issues/financing-the-government/quarterly-refunding
  - Intragovernmental debt (Social Security and other trust funds) is excluded on purpose, because it carries no market rollover risk.

## 2. `US net interest vs national defense outlays FY2015-2026.png` → **Q2 Interest Burden**
- **Title:** US now pays more in interest than on defense: $1.05T vs $965B
- **Caption:** Net interest $1,052B vs defense $965B in FY2026; interest first exceeded defense in FY2024. Source: Treasury MTS Table 9.
- **Label:** DATA
- **Bullets**
  - FY2026 (ended Sep 30 2026): net interest $1,051.8B vs national defense $965.2B.
  - FY2024: interest $881.7B overtook defense ($874.0B) for the first time in this series.
  - FY2015: interest was $223.3B, so it has risen 4.7× in 11 years while defense rose 1.6×.
- **Sources**
  - https://fiscaldata.treasury.gov/datasets/monthly-treasury-statement/outlays-of-the-u-s-government
  - https://api.fiscaldata.treasury.gov/services/api/fiscal_service/v1/accounting/mts/mts_table_9
- **Doubt notes**
  - "Net interest nets out interest the government earns." That's correct: it is the conservative measure. Gross interest, which includes trust funds, is higher still. CBO uses the same net-interest concept: https://www.cbo.gov/topics/budget/federal-debt-and-statutory-limit
  - "Defense excludes Veterans Affairs and DOE nuclear." That is also true; MTS budget function 050 is the standard measure.

## 3. `Average interest rate on marketable Treasury debt vs 10-year yield 2001-2026.png` → **Q2 Interest Burden**
- **Title:** Treasury's average borrowing cost has more than doubled to 3.52%
- **Caption:** Average marketable Treasury rate 3.52% (Sep 2026), up from 1.42% (Jan 2022). Sources: Treasury Fiscal Data, FRED.
- **Label:** DATA
- **Bullets**
  - Sep 2026 average rate: 3.518%. The Jan 2022 low was 1.42%.
  - The 10-year yield was 4.98% on Oct 29 2026, still above the average rate, so as old debt rolls over the average keeps rising.
  - Applied to $31.8T of marketable debt, every +1 pt in the average costs about $318B a year. This is arithmetic, not a forecast.
- **Sources**
  - https://fiscaldata.treasury.gov/datasets/average-interest-rates-treasury-securities/
  - https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/DGS10
- **Doubt notes**
  - "The 10-year isn't what Treasury pays on bills." That's correct, and the 10-year is shown only as the marginal long rate. Bills track the Fed funds rate instead: https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/DTB3

## 4. `Commercial real estate mortgage maturities 2026 by holder.png` → **NEW Q4 Corporate & CRE**
- **Title:** $875B of commercial & multifamily mortgages mature in 2026
- **Caption:** $875B (17% of $5.0T) of CRE mortgages mature in 2026; banks hold $396B. MBA survey, balances at Dec 31 2025.
- **Label:** DATA (industry survey)
- **Bullets**
  - Feb 9 2026, MBA: $875B matures in 2026, after $957B in 2025; $652B is due in 2027.
  - By holder: depositories $396B (21% of their book), CMBS/CLO/ABS $200B (25%), credit companies/warehouse $163B (29%), life insurers $76B (10%) and GSE/FHA $39B (4%).
  - Share of balances maturing by property type: hotel 30%, industrial 23%, office 17%.
- **Sources**
  - https://www.mba.org/news-and-research/newsroom/news/2026/02/09/17-percent-of-commercial-and-multifamily-mortgage-balances-to-mature-in-2026
- **Doubt notes**
  - Part of the 2026 total is 2025 loans that were extended ("extend and pretend"), so the wall keeps rolling forward rather than resolving.
  - This is a survey estimate, not a census.
- **Substitution:** a corporate high-yield maturity wall was not used, because there is no free primary series. Corporate stress is covered by the bank business-loan line in chart 5.

## 5. `US bank loan delinquency rates CRE business cards mortgages 1991-2026.png` → **NEW Q4 Corporate & CRE**
- **Title:** Bank delinquencies: cards 2.85%, CRE up to 1.53% from 2022 lows
- **Caption:** Bank delinquency, Q2 2026: cards 2.85%, CRE 1.53% (0.63% low), business 1.27%. Source: Federal Reserve via FRED.
- **Label:** DATA
- **Bullets**
  - Q2 2026: credit cards 2.85%, single-family mortgages 1.86%, CRE ex-farm 1.53%, business loans 1.27%.
  - The CRE delinquency rate has more than doubled from its Q3 2022 low of 0.63%.
  - For scale, CRE peaked near 8.7% in 2010, so the current level is elevated but far from a GFC-style episode.
- **Sources**
  - https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/DRCRELEXFACBS
  - https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/DRBLACBS
  - https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/DRCCLACBS
  - https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/DRSFRMACBS
  - https://www.federalreserve.gov/releases/chargeoff/
- **Doubt notes**
  - "Bank CRE delinquency understates office stress." That's true. CMBS office delinquency, which is tracked by Trepp, is much higher, but it is proprietary, so it is noted and not charted: https://www.trepp.com/trepptalk/topic/cmbs-delinquency-report
